The train gave up the last of its heat as it pulled into Varn, and [Ada](#char_7f3k2q) was the only passenger who stood.

Twelve years had not moved the station clock. It still ran four minutes fast, as it had when her father set it, so that nobody in Varn would ever miss the last crossing.

From the platform she could see the bridge, the long black spine of it stepping across the river on its nine piers. Someone had tied orange flags to the railings at the second and the seventh. Surveyors' flags.

She stood with her bag at her feet and counted them twice, the way you count stitches in a wound.
