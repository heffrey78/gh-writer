Ben was fourteen, and the river was louder than he had known a river could be.

Their father stood at the seventh pier with a lantern and a spanner as long as his arm, and Tomas held the rope, and Ada, who was sixteen and already better at sums than either of them, read out the gauge every five minutes in a voice that never shook.

Nobody gave Ben anything to hold.

"Go home," his father said. "Tell your mother we're fine."

He went. He told her. It was the last true thing anyone in the family said that night.
